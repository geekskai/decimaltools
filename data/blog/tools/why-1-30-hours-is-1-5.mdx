When **1:30** represents an elapsed duration in hours and minutes (**HH:MM**), it equals **1.5 decimal hours**. The 30 means 30 minutes, while a decimal-hour value uses a base-10 fraction of one hour. Since one hour contains 60 minutes, convert the minutes by dividing by 60:

$$1 + \frac{30}{60} = 1 + 0.5 = 1.5\text{ hours}$$

In HH:MM notation, the two parts are hours and minutes; the digits after a decimal point are tenths or hundredths of an hour. That is why **1:30 is not 1.30 hours**. In fact, 1.30 decimal hours is 1 hour plus 0.30 × 60 = 18 minutes, or **1:18**.

For another example, **2:45** is $2 + 45/60 = 2.75$ hours. Check by converting the decimal part back to minutes: $0.75 × 60 = 45$.

## A repeatable conversion method

For a time written as hours and minutes, use:

`decimal hours = hours + (minutes ÷ 60)`

For hours, minutes, and seconds, use:

`decimal hours = hours + (minutes ÷ 60) + (seconds ÷ 3600)`

Work through the parts separately. For **3:12**, divide 12 by 60 to get 0.2, then add the 3 whole hours: **3.2 hours**. For **0:20:30**, 20 minutes is $20/60 = 0.3333…$ hours and 30 seconds is $30/3600 = 0.00833…$ hours, so the exact total is $41/120$ hours, approximately **0.3417 hours**. The final decimal is rounded here to four places.

The examples use these formats and decimal-hour values:

| Time input | Format | Decimal hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1:30 | HH:MM | 1.5 (exact) |
| 2:45 | HH:MM | 2.75 (exact) |
| 3:12 | HH:MM | 3.2 (exact) |
| 0:20:30 | HH:MM:SS | 0.3417 (rounded to four decimal places) |

## Check the answer in the other direction

Multiply only the fractional part of a decimal hour by 60 to recover minutes. For **1.75 hours**, the whole part is 1 hour and $0.75 × 60 = 45$ minutes, giving **1:45**. If the multiplication produces a fraction of a minute, convert that fraction to seconds by multiplying by 60 again. For example, 0.1 minute is 6 seconds.

This reverse check catches a common mistake: reading the decimal digits as minutes. It also helps distinguish a rounded display from an exact time. Four decimal places may be enough for some calculations, but the needed precision depends on the task and its rounding rules.

## Input formats and boundaries

Colon notation such as `1:30` commonly represents hours and minutes, but longer forms may include seconds, and some systems use a colon for other conventions. Check the labels or interpretation shown by the tool before using a result. A clock time such as 2:30 PM is a time of day; an elapsed duration of 2 hours 30 minutes is a different kind of value.

The linked time to decimal converter currently accepts non-negative input; a negative duration such as `-1:30` is not supported. If another system supports negative durations, follow its signed-time convention. For payroll or billing entries, follow the applicable workplace rules. This conversion explains the arithmetic; it does not determine how an organization rounds, pays, or records time. NIST's [Guide to the SI](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-chapter-5-units-outside-si) states that one hour is 60 minutes and 3,600 seconds.

Common mistakes include dividing minutes by 100, or adding the minute digits directly after a decimal point. Minutes are out of 60, so always calculate `minutes ÷ 60`.
